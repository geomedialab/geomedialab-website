Adèle Clapperton-Richard (they/she) is a postdoctoral fellow in the Department of Geography, Planning, and Environment at Concordia University and lecturer in the Département de géographie at Université Laval. Their current research focuses on Indigenous territorialities, extractive industrial developments, resurgence, and participatory and narrative mapping. Their postdoctoral project aims to reflect on cartographic representations of the entanglements of colonial landscapes with Indigenous cultural landscapes, by mapping the memories and emotions surrounding significant places that have disappeared or been transformed in the Nitassinan (ilnu ancestral territory) of Pessamit. They also combine their illustration practice—ink, watercolor, digital drawing—with their professional projects by diffusing academic knowledge through creative and graphic projects.
